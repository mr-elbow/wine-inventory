🍇 Wine school, one card at a time

Shawn asked what to expect from each bottle — acid, tannins, minerality. Here's the cheat sheet, then every card below puts it into practice.

Acidity — the mouthwatering zing. High acid = crisp, fresh, makes your mouth water (think lemonade). Low acid = soft, round, flabby if too low. Most of this collection is high-acid — that's the natural-wine style.
Tannins — the drying, grippy feeling on your tongue and gums, like strong black tea. Comes from grape skins, seeds, and stems. Big in young reds, almost zero in whites. Chill and food both soften tannins.
Minerality — the stony, salty, chalky, wet-rock character. Scientists argue about whether it's real; drinkers know it when they taste it. Volcanic and limestone soils (Auvergne, Tenerife, Beaujolais) show it most.
Body — how heavy the wine feels in your mouth. Light = skim milk, medium = whole milk, full = cream. Alcohol, fruit ripeness, and oak all add weight.
Skin contact / orange wine — white grapes fermented with their skins, like a red. That's where the amber color, extra texture, and gentle tannin come from. Not a flavor — a method.
Carbonic maceration — whole grapes ferment inside their own skins before crushing, giving bubblegum, banana, and bright cherry notes. The Beaujolais trick — and the secret behind Glou Glou and Fred.
Natural wine — farmed organically, fermented with native yeasts, little or no added sulfur, unfined and unfiltered. Livelier and more variable — drink sooner, keep cool, expect a little funk.
Zero-zero — nothing added, nothing taken away. The most hardcore natural style. Superbloom is one. These are the most fragile bottles in the fridge — drink them first.

🧊 The small-fridge plan

Fourteen bottles, one small fridge. The good news: almost everything here is a drink-now wine. Natural wines with little sulfur don't reward waiting — they reward opening.

How to stack it

Set the fridge to 55°F (13°C) — the one temperature that keeps every style happy. Then give whites and chillable reds a 20–30 minute stint in the kitchen fridge before opening to get them down to serving temp.

TOP SHELF — drink next (coldest spot, front row)
Matervi Tainot (pet nat — keep it ice cold, drink soon) · Gulp Hablo Orange · Superbloom · La Araucaria Rosado · Glou Glou · Gulp Hablo Garnacha — the chillable crew. Serve 45–55°F. These are your Tuesday-to-Friday bottles.
MIDDLE SHELF — the whites
Muscadet Les Prières · Dupeuble Beaujolais Blanc · Aligoté Les Reteurs — serve 45–50°F. Label-out, so you can see at a glance what pairs with tonight's dinner.
BOTTOM / BACK — the waiters (the only ones allowed to linger)
Diochon Moulin-à-Vent · Heinrich Graue Freyheit · Patrick Bouju "P" · Fred #12 · Las Jaras "Steak House" Cab — these can happily sit 6–18 months. Everything else: aim to drink within a year, sooner for the zero-sulfur bottles.
CASE CLOSED — no more mysteries 🔍
Both mystery bottles got names: the village bottle is Matervi "Tainot" and Cowboy Inn is Las Jaras "Steak House" Cab. The only thing still TBD is the Aligoté's producer name — check its back label next time you're in the fridge.

House rules: cork bottles lie on their side (keeps the cork wet); crown-cap and screwcap bottles can stand. Keep the fridge out of direct sun and away from the oven. Don't age natural wine in the kitchen fridge long-term — it's too cold and dry. And keep one empty slot as the "drink next" slot, so the plan survives contact with reality.

🔍 Case closed: the mysteries, solved

Two bottles refused to introduce themselves — no producer or vintage on the front label. A photo search cracked both of them:

  • The village bottle → Matervi "Tainot" 2021. Not a red at all — a sparkling white Pet Nat from Breganze, Veneto, made from Pinot Bianco, Tocai Bianco, and Vespaiola. The dark glass and the sunset-orange label were lying to us.
  • Cowboy Inn → Las Jaras "Steak House" Cabernet Sauvignon 2022. The label photo is Eric Wareheim's own shot of a roadside motel, and it matches Las Jaras' official product photo exactly. Same label photo on the 2023 too, so the back label is the final word on the year.
  • Aligoté "Les Reteurs" 2022 — still the only loose end: the wine is identified (100% Aligoté, Burgundy); only the producer's name is hiding on the back label.
